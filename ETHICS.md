## PRINCIPLES

### 1. Human Agency First
Every system must increase human capability, not replace it.
Automation serves the user. The user does not serve the system.

### 2. Modularity by Design
No component is irreplaceable. Every interface is documented.
Lock-in is a failure mode, not a business model.

### 3. Repairability
Hardware must be repairable. Software must be understandable.
Obsolescence is a design failure.

### 4. Radical Transparency
Decision processes are documented. Data flows are traceable.
Black boxes are tolerated only where absolutely necessary and always audited.

### 5. Privacy as Default
Data belongs to the individual. Encryption is not optional.
Consent is granular, revocable, and informed.

### 6. Interoperability
The system speaks open protocols. It does not create walled gardens.
Integration with external systems is a first-class feature.

### 7. Scientific Integrity
Claims are evidence-based. Research is reproducible.
Hype is a liability. Patience is a virtue.

### 8. Continuous Learning
The system learns from its own operation. Failures are documented.
Successes are questioned. Nothing is final.

### 9. Non-Predatory Economics
Revenue is generated through value creation, not extraction.
Competition is against entropy, not against people.
Pricing is fair. Access is inclusive.

### 10. Inclusive Access
The ecosystem serves all users, not just early adopters.
Capability gaps are bridged, not exploited.

## REVIEW PROCESS

Every major decision is reviewed against these principles by the Governance Steward.
The review is documented in the knowledge base.
No exception is made without explicit justification and community review.
